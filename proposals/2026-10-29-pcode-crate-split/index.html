    Splitting fission-pcode to cut rebuild fan-out (2026-10-29)¶

    Status: proposal. No code is moved by this document.

    This is a mechanical refactor proposal. It makes no decompiler-quality claim (AGENTS.md, Core Rule 10): the only behavioural gate is that DecBench output stays byte-identical (see "Verification").

    Why¶

    A pull request that touches Rust rebuilds every crate above the one it changed. In CI the dependency cache already absorbs the external crates, so the compile time of an ordinary PR is the workspace crates being rebuilt. This proposal asks where that fan-out comes from, and which splits of fission-pcode (137k lines, the crate with the largest own cost) would actually shrink it. It continues ADR 0012, whose phase D ("owner extraction") is still in progress.

    How this was measured (and its limits)¶

    All numbers below were measured on one machine: macOS, rustc 1.97.0, main at 266e3f4a8, cargo test --no-run --workspace --locked --timings with CARGO_INCREMENTAL=0 and CARGO_BUILD_JOBS=4 to resemble CI. The scripts are local (the benchmark/ tree is not tracked); they are described, not committed.

    • Unit time is cargo's per-unit duration from the --timings report, split into lib, lib-test (the crate compiled again with cfg(test)) and other (integration tests, benches, build scripts). It is CPU time summed over units, not wall time.
    • Replay takes the 183 non-merge commits on main from 2026-09-24 to 2026-10-29 that touch Rust or Cargo.toml, maps each touched file to a crate (or to a candidate crate), and charges the unit time of every unit cargo would rebuild: the changed crate, every crate that depends on it, and, through a dev-dependency, only the dependent's test units. A test-only file charges only that crate's test units.
    • Split costs are estimates. The measured fission-pcode cost (108.3 s: lib 41, lib-test 66, other 1) is divided over the parts by line count (lib-test also carries the 30,419 lines of in-crate tests, which belong to the remainder). The three parts sum back to the measured 108.3 s.
    • The replay models the leaf as the 6,559 lines of pcode, arch and cfg (5.7 s) and fission-cspec as the 5,127 lines of cspec (4.4 s). The extra midend/cfg.rs (1,209 lines) and abi.rs (597 lines) listed under the candidates would add roughly 1 s and 0.5 s; they do not change the ranking.
    • Not modelled: nextest execution time (CI: 258 s for 3,270 tests), link time (small here: 8 s over 85 links, but this is macOS ld, not the Linux linker CI uses), and the wall-clock effect of fewer units on a 4-vCPU runner.
    • A commit is treated as one CI build. Cargo rebuilds a dependent on any source change of its dependency, including private changes, so no saving is assumed from "the interface did not change".

    Observed baseline¶

    Where the time is¶

    Cold build, CI-like After touching fission-pcode
    Wall time 232 s 92 s
    Unit time, workspace crates 519 s 305 s
    Unit time, external crates 389 s 0 s (cached)
    Linking 172 links, 11.9 s (1.3 %) 85 links, 8.0 s (2.6 %)

    External dependencies are not the lever for an ordinary PR; the workspace crates are. fission-pcode alone is 108.3 s of the 519 s.

    What the history changes, and how far it propagates¶

    Replaying the 183 commits (rebuild-on-change = unit time rebuilt when that crate's source changes):

    Crate Commits touching it Rebuild-on-change Share of total replay cost
    fission-midend-normalize 35 389 s (75 %) 18 %
    fission-loader 24 472 s (91 %) 15 %
    fission-pcode 31 350 s (68 %) 14 %
    fission-signatures 13 493 s (95 %) 8 %
    fission-sleigh 18 309 s (60 %) 7 %
    fission-static 19 289 s (56 %) 7 %
    fission-midend-prehir 12 413 s (80 %) 6 %
    fission-midend-structuring 12 370 s (71 %) 6 %
    fission-midend-core 9 420 s (81 %) 5 %
    fission-emulator 23 136 s (26 %) 4 %

    Two structural facts explain why fission-pcode is on the critical path of so many rebuilds:

    1. Crates that only need the IR still depend on the whole crate. fission-sleigh, fission-emulator, fission-dir and fission-static import almost nothing from fission-pcode except p-code IR types (below), but because they depend on the crate, a change in the 114k-line midend rebuilds them. fission-emulator and fission-dir additionally reach fission-pcode through fission-static and fission-decompiler.
    2. A dev-dependency cycle (fission-pcode dev-depends on fission-sleigh and fission-static, which depend on fission-pcode). Cargo allows it, but it means a change in sleigh, static, or anything beneath them rebuilds fission-pcode's test units (lib-test is 66 s, larger than the 41 s lib, because of the 30,419 lines of in-crate tests).

    29 % of the replayed commits (53 of 183) touch a crate with ten or more dependents (core, loader, signatures, midend-core, midend-prehir). Splitting fission-pcode cannot help those; see "Beyond this proposal".

    Dependent IR types (pcode) cfg midend / render
    fission-sleigh 43 4 (AddressCfgSnapshot, AddressEdge) 0
    fission-emulator 41 (ir) 0 0

    fission-decompiler 9 0 29: cspec, PreHir*, build_raw_hir, NirFunctionHints, …

    Only fission-decompiler genuinely needs the pipeline. The other four need the IR, the address-CFG snapshot, and (for static) a handful of cspec/abi helpers.

    Candidates¶

    Names are working names.

    A. fission-pcode-ir: the p-code IR leaf¶

    • Contents: src/pcode (5,946), src/arch (270), src/cfg (343) and midend/cfg.rs (1,209), about 7.8k lines.
    • Why it is clean: pcode and arch have no workspace imports; src/pcode has one pub(crate) item and no deep paths used from midend or render.
    • Prerequisites: (1) cfg/export.rs calls midend::structuring_cfg_edges, which lives in midend/cfg.rs; moving both resolves it. (2) midend/cfg.rs calls one builder function (resolve_lifted_direct_call_target); relocate or copy that helper. (3) fission-pcode re-exports the moved items under the same paths (fission_pcode::{Pcode*, Varnode, arch, cfg, ir}), so the five dependents keep compiling unchanged.
    • Fan-out effect alone: the leaf (estimated 5.7 s) no longer rebuilds when a lower-layer crate changes. Replay: 4.7 % less rebuild work (see table). By itself it is weak, because sleigh, emulator and dir still reach fission-pcode through static and decompiler.
    • Risk: low. Public paths are preserved by re-exports. Watch for private helpers shared with midend (none found), #![forbid(unsafe_code)] and serde derives in the new crate, and benches/examples that import by path.

    B. fission-cspec: calling-convention / register model¶

    • Contents: midend/cspec (5,127), midend/abi.rs (597, infer_entry_register_param_arity) and seed_nir_render_options (midend/mod.rs). About 5.7k lines plus a small function.
    • Dependencies: fission-midend-core (NirRenderOptions), fission-loader (LoadedBinary), the leaf from A; RegisterNamer stays re-exported.
    • Prerequisite and ADR impact: ADR 0012 says seed_nir_render_options and the other p-code adapters remain in fission-pcode. This candidate moves one of them (not nir_admission_facts_from_pcode), so the ADR needs an amendment.
    • Fan-out effect alone: small, about 0.6 % beyond A (its 4.4 s estimated cost). Its value is that it is what lets fission-static stop depending on fission-pcode (candidate S).
    • Risk: medium. New dependency on the loader from a crate that today sits inside fission-pcode; cspec behaviour feeds register naming everywhere, so the byte-identical gate must be run on both O0 and O2 rows.

    S. Decouple fission-static from fission-pcode (requires A and B)¶

    fission-static uses 29 IR references and the eight helpers listed above. After A and B every one of them lives in the leaf or fission-cspec, so static (including its dev-dependency on fission-pcode) can depend on those two crates instead. This is a mechanical import change.

    • Why it matters: static is on the path to sleigh (dev), emulator and dir; cutting it removes them from the rebuild set of a fission-pcode change.
    • Fan-out effect (A + B + S): a change in fission-pcode rebuilds 175 s instead of 350 s, and only cli, decompiler, dir and pcode (before: also dynamic, emulator, script, sleigh, static). Replay: 19.5 % less rebuild work, median commit 350 s → 213 s.
    • Risk: medium. Pays off only together with A and B. If a helper turns out to need PreviewBuilder internals, S is partly blocked; cargo check -p fission-static after the import switch is the test.

    F. Remove fission-pcode's dev-dependencies on sleigh and static¶

    Only one file uses them: crates/fission-pcode/tests/cfg_ghidra_parity_fixtures.rs. Moving it into crates/fission-sleigh/tests/ (which already dev-depends on static) removes both dev edges. With A done it needs only the leaf (AddressCfgSnapshot, structuring_cfg_edges).

    • Fan-out effect alone: 3.2 % less rebuild work: a change in sleigh stops rebuilding fission-pcode's 66 s test unit (309 s → 242 s per change). It adds to A+B+S: 22.4 % in total.
    • Risk: low. One file, test-only. A dev cycle between sleigh and static remains and is out of scope.

    Considered and not recommended¶

    • Extract render (16,099 lines). render and midend call each other (25 and 8 references), and fission-pcode would still depend on a render crate, so a render change would rebuild fission-pcode anyway: no fan-out gain.
    • Extract builder (67,301 lines). structuring and pass call back into it (2 + 2 references), PreviewBuilder implements StructuringHost, and fission-decompiler needs the pipeline anyway, so no dependent would be isolated. High risk, no measured gain.
    • Move the 30,419 in-crate test lines to tests/. Each file becomes its own test binary with its own link, and the tests use crate-private items.

    Combined effect (model, 183 commits)¶

    Candidates Total rebuild work Mean / commit Median p90 Saved vs now
    none (current) 57,327 s 313.3 s 350.4 s 472.3 s
    A 54,617 s 298.5 s 344.5 s 446.4 s 4.7 %
    A + B 54,289 s 296.7 s 340.0 s 446.4 s 5.3 %
    F 55,517 s 303.4 s 350.4 s 472.3 s 3.2 %
    A + B + S 46,156 s 252.2 s 213.2 s 446.4 s 19.5 %
    A + B + S + F 44,498 s 243.2 s 213.2 s 446.4 s 22.4 %
    • The p90 does not move: commits that touch the bottom of the graph (core, loader, signatures, midend-*) rebuild almost everything in every variant.
    • These are model results on estimated split costs. They are an argument for the order of work, not a promise: the success criterion below is a re-measurement.
    • The saving is in compile unit time. CI test selection is by affected crate, so a smaller affected set should also shorten test execution; that was not measured.

    Beyond this proposal¶

    Splitting fission-pcode addresses about a fifth of the replay cost at best. The rest sits lower: fission-midend-normalize (18 %), fission-loader (15 %) and fission-signatures (8 %) are changed often and almost everything depends on them. Before proposing anything there, check what those commits actually touch (for example whether loader or signatures changes are data/format code that the pipeline above does not read), as a separate measured proposal.

    Order of work¶

    One crate per pull request, each independently revertable:

    1. F (one file, no dependencies on the others). Re-measure.
    2. A, with re-exports so no dependent changes. Re-measure.
    3. B, with the ADR 0012 amendment.
    4. S: switch fission-static's imports. This is the step that moves the numbers; if it is blocked, stop and reassess before B is worth keeping.

    Verification¶

    For every step:

    • Behaviour unchanged: the 250-row DecBench sample-set gate, run with FISSION_BENCHMARK_NO_CACHE=1 on a release fission_cli built from main and from the change, must give byte-identical per-function dumps. This is the method used for the CircleRange change. The gate needs the eval kit under vendor/decbench-evalkit/.
    • cargo check --workspace, cargo nextest run for the affected crates (both the dev and test profiles), cargo fmt --all --check.
    • Effect re-measured, not assumed: rerun the --timings build and the commit replay with the new crate graph and report the actual numbers next to the model's. No saving is claimed before that.
    • Update ADR 0012, docs/PROJECT_MAP.md, and the child AGENTS.md files that name crates/fission-pcode/src/... paths.

    Open questions¶

    • Are infer_entry_register_param_arity and seed_nir_render_options the only midend items fission-static needs? The import scan says yes for src/ and tests/; cargo check after the switch is the proof.
    • Does midend/cfg.rs have users inside midend that would need the leaf crate as a new dependency edge? (builder uses it ×11; that edge goes pcode → leaf, which is the intended direction.)
    • Is a smaller lib-test achievable without moving tests? The 30k lines of in-crate tests are why fission-pcode's test unit is 1.6× its library.

    Results after F, A, B and S (measured 2026-10-29)¶

    Steps F (#694), A (#695), B (#696) and S (#697) were implemented, each with a byte-identical sample-set dump (233 rows, O0 and O2) and an unchanged nextest count. These are mechanical changes; none of them is a decompiler-quality claim.

    What differed from the plan

    • midend/cfg.rs moved only in part (the p-code-only block CFG functions, about 760 lines with tests). The LSDA, no-return and PreHir-expression parts need the loader, call summaries or midend types and stayed, so the resolve_lifted_direct_call_target back-edge never arose. Total moved into the leaf: about 7.3k lines.
    • abi.rs moved only in part: infer_entry_register_param_arity. AbiState and the providers are bound to builder types. The address-space ids and the register/unique varnode predicates, shared by both sides, went to the leaf instead.
    • S also switched fission-sleigh, fission-emulator and fission-dir (not just fission-static) to the leaf. Without that, sleigh and emulator still reached fission-pcode and the fan-out did not shrink. The answer to the first open question is yes: cargo check after the import switch passes. Only fission-decompiler now depends on fission-pcode directly (dir through a dev edge, cli through the decompiler).

    Measured rebuild fan-out (CI-like: CARGO_INCREMENTAL=0, CARGO_BUILD_JOBS=4, cargo test --no-run --workspace --locked --timings; one touch of a single source file after a cold build, one run per cell, so the wall times carry run-to-run noise):

    Touched file Crates whose units rebuilt, before after Wall, before after Summed unit time, before after
    fission-pcode/src/midend/structuring/mod.rs sleigh, emulator, cli, pcode, static, decompiler, script, dynamic, dir pcode, cli, decompiler, dir 114 s 45 s 372 s 128 s
    fission-sleigh/src/lib.rs pcode, emulator, sleigh, cli, static, decompiler, dir, script, dynamic emulator, sleigh, cli, static, decompiler, dir, script, dynamic 83 s 62 s 307 s 211 s
    p-code IR module (pcode, now pcode-ir) everything above everything above plus the two new crates 92 s 86 s 329 s 318 s
    cspec module everything above same, cspec itself small 92 s 78 s 311 s 263 s
    fission-static/src/lib.rs emulator, cli, pcode, static, sleigh, decompiler, script, dynamic, dir emulator, cli, static, sleigh, decompiler, script, dynamic, dir 72 s 60 s 226 s 201 s
    • The case the split targets, an edit inside the midend, drops from nine rebuilt crates to four and from 114 s to 45 s of wall time. That is the 175 s versus 350 s direction the model predicted, at a smaller absolute scale because the cold-build dependency cost is cached here.
    • Edits at the bottom (the IR itself) gain almost nothing: everything depends on them, and the split adds two small crates to the rebuild.
    • CI test selection (scripts/ci/select_affected_crates.py) for a midend edit shrinks from cli, decompiler, dir, dynamic, emulator, pcode, script, sleigh, static to cli, decompiler, dir, pcode. A fission-sleigh or fission-static edit no longer selects fission-pcode (tests that were 66 s of unit time).

    Not measured: the 183-commit replay was not rerun on the new graph, so the 19.5 % figure remains the model's. The wall times above are single runs. Actual CI wall time on pull requests has not been observed yet; compare it over the next merges before claiming a CI speed-up.
